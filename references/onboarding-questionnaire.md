# Structured Onboarding

Never open with "tell me everything". Present only the questions still unanswered, compactly. Allow short phrases and partial answers. Skip anything inferable from context. Do not use technical AI jargon.

| # | Topic | Ask | Examples to offer |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mission | What do you want this team to help you accomplish? What problem or outcome? | |
| 2 | Top priorities | Your two or three most important goals, highest first | |
| 3 | Scope | What should the team be responsible for doing? | research, finding opportunities, winning clients, projects, content, operations, relationships, monitoring |
| 4 | Audience / environment | Who or what does the team work with? | individuals, businesses, industries, communities, markets, locations |
| 5 | Deliverables | What should the team produce or achieve? | clients, revenue, applications, reports, content, partnerships, projects |
| 6 | Execution authority | How much should the team do on its own? | research and recommend / prepare for my approval / execute approved actions / handle routine work and escalate important decisions |
| 7 | Resources and tools | Accounts, files, platforms available? (If unsure, I will check the platform.) | email, calendar, CRM, social, website, databases, task tools |
| 8 | Constraints | What should the team avoid? | budget, geography, time, approval-needed actions, prohibited methods |
| 9 | Success | What would make this a success? | numbers, outcomes, visibility, time saved |
| 10 | Reporting | How should the team report to you? | daily, weekly, milestones, important developments only, on demand |

## Rules
- Do not force an answer to every question.
- Ask follow-ups only where a missing answer would materially change the organisation.
- If a vague answer arrives, structure it for the user instead of asking them to rewrite it.

## Turning answers into a mission
1. State the core mission in one or two sentences.
2. Consolidate related priorities; separate strategic goals from operational activities.
3. List expected outcomes and scope.
4. Note adjacent opportunities naturally connected to the mission.
5. Record constraints and approval requirements.
6. Propose preliminary success indicators.
Present a concise mission summary. If nothing material is ambiguous, proceed straight to team design.
