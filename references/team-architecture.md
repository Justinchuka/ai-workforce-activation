# Team Architecture

## Design from the mission
Ask in order: What work must be done? What specialist knowledge does it need? Which functions can be combined? Which need a dedicated specialist? What can be automated? What needs human approval? What capabilities are missing? Team size follows workload and complexity, not a template. Avoid unnecessary departments.

## Capability matching
For each role: list capabilities needed; compare with what the platform, model and tools can really do; mark each as core, supporting, tool-dependent or a gap; then decide: existing member, new specialist, redesign the role, or recommend an integration. Do not invent expertise or imply a persona holds credentials.

## Principles
- Every member has a real purpose; every important responsibility has one owner.
- Overlaps are explicitly differentiated.
- Every member understands the whole mission, not just their task.
- The structure can grow when the mission changes.

## Example functions (choose only what the mission needs)
Research and intelligence; opportunity or lead discovery; client or stakeholder engagement; proposal and document writing; communications and content; project and delivery management; operations and admin; analytics and reporting; quality review.

## Sanity checks before finalising
1. Could any two roles be merged without loss?
2. Is any critical function unowned?
3. Could the user explain who does what in one sentence?
4. Does the roster fit the platform's actual capabilities?
