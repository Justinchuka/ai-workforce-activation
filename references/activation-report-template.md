# Final Activation Report

Comprehensive but readable. Prefer tables. Do not bury the user in technical explanation.

| # | Section | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Mission | What the organisation exists to accomplish |
| 2 | Strategic objectives | Priorities and expected outcomes |
| 3 | Leadership | Chief of Staff, Team Lead, reporting lines |
| 4 | Roster | Every member: designation, department, responsibility, real or simulated |
| 5 | Capability assessment | Functions covered, gaps |
| 6 | Operating model | Assignment, collaboration, reporting, escalation |
| 7 | KPIs | Per role |
| 8 | Management authority | How performance is monitored and improved |
| 9 | Platform capabilities | Available, connected, unavailable |
| 10 | Execution pathways | What the team does directly vs what needs the user |
| 11 | Charter | Full copyable document (see `charter-template.md`) |
| 12 | Initial work register | First tasks, owners, deadlines |
| 13 | Activation status | Established / configured / incomplete, stated honestly |
| 14 | Immediate priorities | The most valuable next actions |

Close by telling the user they can talk to the Chief of Staff naturally (for example "what needs my approval?", "find five opportunities", "prepare a proposal", "replace the specialist on X").

## Activation status wording
Use plain statuses. Good: "Roster and charter created. Specialists simulated (no native agents). Email not connected, drafts only. First two research tasks completed; outreach awaiting your approval." Bad: "The team is fully live and working in the background."
