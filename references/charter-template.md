# Organisational Charter Template

The charter is the source of truth: constitution, operating manual, decision register, changelog and institutional memory. Output it as copyable Markdown. Update only on **material** change (new or removed role, changed mission or priority, new decision, new tool, new approval rule). Do not issue a new version for trivial chat.

```markdown
# CHARTER: <Organisation name>
Version: <n.n> | Updated: <date> | Owner: <user>

## 1. Identity and mission
<Purpose in 1-3 sentences>

## 2. Strategic objectives
| # | Objective | Success measure | Priority |

## 3. Operating principles
<Short list, user-specific>

## 4. Leadership and reporting
User → Chief of Staff → Team Lead → Specialists (adjust)

## 5. Team roster
| Name | Designation | Dept | Primary responsibility | Reports to | KPIs | Real or simulated |

## 6. Workflows and reporting
<Assignment flow, report format, cadence, escalation triggers>

## 7. Decision rights and approvals
| Action | Who decides | Standing authority? |

## 8. Platform and tools
| Capability | Status (available / setup / permission / unsupported) | Manual fallback |

## 9. Constraints and boundaries

## 10. Current projects
<Links to work register>

## 11. Bottlenecks and open questions

## 12. Decision history
| Date | Decision | Rationale | Status (Current / SUPERSEDED) |

## 13. Change log
| Version | Date | Summary | Detail |
```

## Version control rules
Every material update records: version, date, summary, detailed changes, previous vs current decisions, superseded decisions, rationale, open questions. Never silently erase a decision; mark it SUPERSEDED.

## Continuity
When a conversation gets long or restarts: provide the latest full charter, carry forward the register, outstanding assignments, decisions and team structure. Never promise perfect memory. If persistent memory is unavailable or uncertain, tell the user to save the charter and paste it into the next session. If the latest charter is missing, ask for it; do not invent history.
